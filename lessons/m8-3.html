The question paper before the exam

Before board exams, students collect old question papers. They do not read them like a story. They pick a paper, set a clock, write the answers, then check the book. After a few papers, every new paper feels like a friend.

This lesson is your old question paper for system design. It has 50 questions. Every question points to the lesson that teaches the answer, so when you get stuck you know exactly where to look.

The 50 questions

The questions are in three groups: Easy (the basic ideas, 14 questions), Medium (trade-offs and the classic problems, 21 questions) and Hard (full designs, failure handling and AI, 15 questions). They cover high-level design (HLD), low-level design (LLD) and AI design. Tags: [HLD], [LLD], [AI]. A full design question needs a 45-minute round (m4-1); a concept question needs about 2 to 5 minutes.

How to use the bank, and how not to

  • Do answer aloud. Silent thinking feels like understanding but is not. You will find the gaps only when you speak.
  • Do use the seven steps (m4-2) for every full design question, even a rough version.
  • Do repeat the questions you marked red. Repeating is the whole point.
  • Don't memorise answers word by word. Interviewers change the question slightly, and memorised answers break.
  • Don't only practise your favourite groups. If you love HLD, force yourself to do the [LLD] and [AI] ones.

Easy questions are 2-5 minute talks. Do not spend 45 minutes on them. Medium and Hard design questions are full rounds, so practise a 10-minute version first (requirements, estimate, boxes, one trade-off), then a full 45 minutes once a week.

Do it yourself (set it up in 20 minutes, then 40 minutes a day)

A. Make your tracking sheet

  1. Open a new tab, type sheets.google.com and press Enter. Click Blank spreadsheet (it may say Blank).
  2. Click Untitled spreadsheet at the top left, type Question bank tracker and press Enter.
  3. In row 1, click each cell and type these headings, pressing Tab after each: No, Group, Date, Minutes, Colour, Gap to fix.
  4. In column A, type the numbers 1 to 50 down rows 2 to 51. Tip: type 1 and 2 in the first two cells, select both, and drag the small blue square at the corner downward.
  5. In column B, write Easy for rows 2-15, Medium for rows 16-36, and Hard for rows 37-51.

B. A daily session (the 10-minute loop)

  1. Pick three questions: one Easy, one Medium, one Hard or [AI]. Choose by number so you do not always take your favourites. For example, use the day of the month as a starting point.
  2. Press the ⊞ Win key, type Clock, press Enter, click Timer, set 10 minutes and click ▶.
  3. Answer the question out loud. For a full design, say the requirements, a rough estimate, the boxes (draw them in Excalidraw) and one trade-off. For a concept question, give the idea, an example, and a trade-off.
  4. When the timer rings, open the linked lesson and compare. What did you miss? Write it in the "Gap to fix" column in a few words.
  5. Colour column E: green if you covered the main points, amber if half, red if you got stuck. To colour a cell, select it, click the paint-bucket icon in the toolbar and pick a colour.
  6. Next day, start with any red questions before choosing new ones.

C. A weekly full round

  1. Once a week, pick one Medium or Hard design question and do the full 45 minutes with the timer, using the 7-step framework.
  2. Record the date and "45" in the Minutes column. You should see your green count grow over the weeks.

Quick quiz

Why answer aloud instead of in your head?

Speaking exposes gaps you cannot see silently, and it trains the exact skill the interview tests: explaining while thinking.

Why not memorise full answers?

Interviewers change the question, add constraints or ask a follow-up. Understanding the reasons adapts; a memorised script breaks.

What do you do with a red question?

Re-read the linked lesson, write the gap in the sheet, and retry it the next day before new questions.

You have only 30 minutes today. What do you pick?

Three Easy or Medium concept questions with a 5 to 10 minute timer each, or one full design in a 10-minute short version. Skip a full 45 minutes until you have a longer day.

Which question tags does the bank use?

[HLD] high-level design, [LLD] low-level design and [AI] AI design.

Say it in the interview

"I will start by clarifying requirements, then estimate the scale, then draw the high-level boxes, and then dive into the one part that is hardest."
"There are two options here. I would pick this one because of that, and the cost is this. If the numbers change, I would switch."

Common follow-up question: "What would you change if traffic grew ten times?" Model answer: "I would find the first bottleneck, usually the database, then add the cheapest fix such as caching or a read replica, and only then consider sharding. I would also add monitoring so I can see it before users do."

The night before the board exam

The night before an exam, a smart student does not learn new chapters. She opens her short notes, revises the big formulas, packs her pen, admit card and water bottle, and sleeps early. A tired student who stayed up cramming forgets even what he knew.

You are at that stage. You have finished eight modules. What is left is not learning more, but revising in the right order, getting your things ready, and arriving fresh.

The checklist and the 14-day plan

Pre-interview checklist

Read this list a day before and again on the morning. Tick each item in your head. If you cannot, the link shows where to fix it.

  • Method: I can say the seven steps without notes (m4-2) and I know how the 45 minutes flow (m4-1).
  • Estimation: I can work out requests per second and storage in under five minutes (m3-9) and I know the latency numbers (m1-5).
  • Building blocks: I can explain a load balancer, cache, queue, replica and shard in one sentence each, with a trade-off (m2-5, m2-7, m2-11, m2-9, m2-10).
  • Big ideas: I can explain CAP, consistency and idempotency with an example (m3-1, m3-2, m3-4).
  • Classic designs: I have drawn at least five from memory (Module 5).
  • LLD: I can explain SOLID and name three patterns with use cases (m6-2, m6-3).
  • AI: I can walk through RAG and say how I would control cost and quality (m7-3, m7-4, m7-6).
  • Stories: I have two project stories that take three minutes each (m8-1).
  • Company: I know the likely rounds for this company type (m8-2) and I asked the recruiter about the format.
  • Practice: I did at least two timed mocks and my sheet has more green than red (m8-3, m8-4).
  • Setup: Internet works, laptop is charged, Excalidraw opens, camera and microphone tested, a quiet room, water nearby, link and time double-checked.
  • Mind: I slept well. I will say "let me think for a moment" instead of panicking. I will ask clarifying questions first.

14-day revision plan (2 hours a day)

Each day: about 60 minutes re-reading the key points (not the full lessons), 45 minutes on the practice task, 15 minutes writing what is still weak. Tick off the days in your notes. If you miss a day, do not double up; just continue and drop the lowest-priority task.

If your interview is sooner or later than 14 days

  • Fewer days: keep days 6, 12 and 13 always. Cut Module 1 and 2 revision to your weakest topics, and use the question bank colours to choose.
  • More days: repeat the Module 5 days with different problems and add one full mock per week.

Do and don't

  • Do revise from your own short notes and drawings, not by re-reading whole lessons.
  • Do protect day 14. Rest is part of the plan.
  • Don't start a new tool or topic in the last three days.
  • Don't compare your progress with others. Compare it with your own sheet last week.

Do it yourself (about 30 minutes now, then daily)

A. Make your plan sheet

  1. Open sheets.google.com and click Blank spreadsheet (it may say Blank). Name it 14-day plan by clicking the title at the top left.
  2. In row 1 type: Day, Date, Lessons, Task, Done, Weak spot.
  3. In column A type 1 to 14 down rows 2 to 15.
  4. Click cell B2 and type your start date, for example 27 Oct. In B3 type the next day. Select both and drag the small blue square downward to fill the dates. If Sheets turns them into something odd, click the cell and use Format then Number then Date.
  5. Copy the lessons and task for each day from the table above into columns C and D. Shorten them if you like.
  6. Click Insert then Checkbox after selecting column E rows 2 to 15. You should see little boxes you can tick.

B. Each day

  1. Start with your weak spots from yesterday (10 minutes).
  2. Skim the day's lessons using only the headings, the picture and the quiz. Open the full text only for what you cannot answer.
  3. Do the practice task with a timer from the Windows Clock app (press ⊞ Win, type Clock).
  4. Tick the checkbox and write the one weakest thing in column F.

C. The night before

  1. Read the checklist above once. Anything not true goes in a single sentence in your notes; do not start fixing it tonight.
  2. Open Excalidraw and check it loads. Test your camera, microphone and internet. Charge your laptop and put the charger near you.
  3. Set an alarm. Close the laptop an hour before bed.

Quick quiz

Why is day 14 a rest day?

A tired mind recalls less and thinks slowly. Rest before the interview helps more than another hour of revision.

You missed two days of the plan. What do you do?

Continue from today and drop the lowest-priority task. Do not double up days, because it makes the work shallow.

Which days stay even if you only have a week?

Days 6 (a full dry run), 12 (stories and question bank) and 13 (mocks).

Name three things from the setup part of the checklist.

Internet and charger, Excalidraw working, camera and microphone tested, a quiet room, and the link and time double-checked.

Would you learn a new topic two days before the interview? Why or why not?

No. New topics stay shallow and can shake your confidence. Use the time to revise your weak spots and rest.

Say it in the interview

"I have prepared for this with a structured method, and I will walk through it step by step so you can follow my thinking."
"I am not sure about this part. Let me reason from first principles and tell you my assumptions."

Common follow-up question: "What did you do to prepare?" Model answer: "I revised the building blocks and trade-offs, practised classic designs aloud with a timer, prepared stories from my own projects, and did timed mock interviews with a scoring sheet."

Drawing the map before the trip

Before a school trip, the teacher draws a map on the board: "Bus here, lunch there, toilets here." Everyone understands it in one look, faster than any long speech.

In a system design interview you do the same thing. You draw boxes (computers and programs) and arrows (messages between them). The drawing is your answer. So you need to draw quickly and neatly, and that takes practice.

Excalidraw: a free whiteboard

Excalidraw is a free drawing website. Its drawings look hand-made, which is exactly what interviewers expect. Many companies even use it in their online interviews. It works in the browser: no install, no account.

You only need five tools. Each one has a keyboard key, and using the keys makes you fast:

ToolKeyUsed for
Select / moveV or 1Click things to pick them, drag them to move
RectangleR or 2Servers, services, apps
EllipseO or 4Users, or start/end points
ArrowA or 5"This sends a message to that"
TextT or 8Labels

Other handy keys: Ctrl + Z undo · Ctrl + D duplicate the selected shape · Delete removes the selected shape · hold Shift while drawing for perfectly straight arrows and perfect squares · hold the Space bar and drag to move around the page · Ctrl + mouse wheel to zoom.

There is a full list of shortcuts inside Excalidraw: press ? (that is Shift + /).

Draw it yourself (about 30 minutes)

A. Open Excalidraw

  1. New tab: Ctrl + T. Type excalidraw.com and press Enter.
  2. A big white page opens with a toolbar at the top. If a welcome message covers the middle, just click anywhere on the white page and it goes away.
  3. Bookmark it: Ctrl + D → Done.

B. Draw the client box

  1. Press R (rectangle tool).
  2. On the left side of the page, press and hold the left mouse button, drag down-right to make a box, then let go.
  3. With the box still selected, double-click inside it. A blinking cursor appears in the middle.
  4. Type Your phone (client). Press Esc to stop typing.

C. Make the server box (the fast way)

  1. Press V. Click your box once to select it (blue handles appear around it).
  2. Press Ctrl + D. A copy appears. Drag the copy to the right side of the page.
  3. Double-click the text inside the copy. Press Ctrl + A to select the old text, and type Server. Press Esc.

D. Draw the arrows

  1. Press A (arrow tool).
  2. Move the mouse to the right edge of the phone box. Press and hold, then hold Shift too (for a straight line), drag to the left edge of the server box, and let go. The arrow "sticks" to both boxes; if you move a box later, the arrow follows.
  3. Double-click the middle of the arrow and type 1. Please give me the page. Press Esc.
  4. Draw a second arrow from the server back to the phone, a little lower. Label it 2. Here is the page.
  5. Make the second arrow dashed: press V, click the arrow, then in the panel on the left find Stroke style and click the dashed line.

E. Save it to your computer

Excalidraw remembers your drawing in the browser, but clearing your history erases it. Always save the drawing as a file.
  1. Click the ☰ menu (three lines, top left).
  2. Click Save to… and then Save to disk. (On some versions it is just Save as.)
  3. A Windows "Save" window opens. Pick your notes folder (you make it in the next lesson; for now, Documents is fine). In File name, type 00 client-server. Click Save.
  4. To open it later: ☰ → Open (or Ctrl + O), click your file, then Open.

F. Export a picture (to share or put in notes)

  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + E (or ☰ → Export image…).
  2. Click PNG. The picture is saved to your Downloads folder.

G. Speed challenge

  1. Clear the page: ☰ → Reset the canvas → Confirm. (You already saved the file, so nothing is lost.)
  2. Open the Windows Clock: press the ⊞ Win key, type Clock, press Enter, click Stopwatch, then click ▶.
  3. Redraw the whole picture from memory, using only keyboard keys to switch tools. Aim for under 2 minutes. Do it 3 times.

Optional: Excalidraw has free shape libraries with server, database and cloud icons. Click the Library button (a book icon, top right) → Browse libraries, search for "system design" or "architecture", and click Add to Excalidraw. Plain boxes with clear labels are perfectly fine in interviews, though.

Quick quiz

Which key gives you the arrow tool?

A (or 5).

How do you write text inside a box?

Double-click inside the box, then type. Press Esc when done.

Why save to disk when Excalidraw already remembers your drawing?

Because it only remembers it in the browser. Clearing history or switching computers loses it. A file is safe.

In the picture, which side is the client?

The phone. The client is the one that asks; the server is the one that answers.

Why this matters

In online interviews you are often asked to "share your screen and draw". Interviewers notice when someone fumbles with the tool. Being fast lets you spend your time thinking, not hunting for buttons. A clean diagram with clear labels already says "this person is organised".

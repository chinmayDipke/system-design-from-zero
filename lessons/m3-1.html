Two branches of one bank, and a cut phone line

A bank has two branches in two towns. Both keep a notebook with your balance: Rs 1000. Every time you deposit or withdraw, the branch phones the other branch so both notebooks match.

One day the phone line between the branches is cut. You walk into branch 1 and ask to withdraw Rs 800. Branch 1 cannot reach branch 2. It has two choices. It can say "Sorry, come back when the line works" (safe, but you are turned away). Or it can hand you the cash anyway, and hope nobody withdraws from branch 2 at the same time (you are served, but the notebooks may now disagree).

There is no third choice that is perfect. That is the whole CAP theorem.

C, A and P, and what you really choose

You met replication in the replication lesson: the same data lives on several machines. CAP describes what happens to such a system when the machines cannot talk to each other.

The three letters

LetterMeaningPlain words
ConsistencyEvery read sees the latest write (or an error)All branches show the same balance
AvailabilityEvery request to a working node gets a real answerNo customer is turned away
Partition toleranceThe system keeps working even when the network between nodes breaksThe phone line is cut, the branches still open

Why P is not optional

A partition is when some nodes cannot reach others: a cable cut, a switch fails, a data centre is overloaded. On a real network over many machines, this will happen sometimes. You cannot "choose" a network that never breaks. So the theorem, in practice, says: when a partition happens, you must choose between C and A.

  • CP (consistent, not always available): during the partition, refuse or delay requests that might return stale or conflicting data. The branch says "come back later".
  • AP (available, maybe inconsistent): keep answering, accept that nodes may disagree for a while, and fix it up after the partition heals. The branch hands out the cash.

When there is no partition (most of the time), you can have both C and A. CAP only talks about the bad moment.

CP vs AP examples

FeaturePickWhy
Bank balance, money transferCPA wrong balance costs real money. Better to say "try again".
Likes count on a postAPIf one person sees 1,203 and another 1,204 for a minute, nobody is hurt.

PACELC: the part CAP forgot

CAP says nothing about normal days. PACELC adds it: if there is a Partition, choose A or C; Else (no partition), choose Latency or Consistency.

Why is there a trade-off on a normal day? To make every replica agree before answering, the system must wait for messages between machines (see replication: synchronous vs asynchronous). Waiting costs time. Answering from the nearest replica without waiting is fast but may be a little stale. So "strongly consistent" usually means "slower".

StyleDuring partitionNormal day
PC/EC (e.g. a strict bank ledger)ConsistencyConsistency, accept slower
PA/EL (e.g. a likes counter, a feed)AvailabilityLow latency, accept stale

Many databases let you tune this per query. The next lesson, consistency models, shows how.

Common myths

  • "Pick any 2 of 3." Misleading. P is forced on you, so the real choice is C or A, and only during a partition.
  • "AP means data is lost." No. It means replicas may disagree for a while and then converge.
  • "A whole system is either CP or AP." No. Different features in one app choose differently: payments CP, likes AP.
  • "CAP's availability = 99.99% uptime." No. In CAP, "available" means every working node answers every request. It is a strict idea, not an uptime number (see availability and nines).
  • "A single-node Postgres is CA." It has no partition to worry about between nodes, so the question does not arise until you add replicas.

Trade-offs

  • CP: correct data, but some users see errors or timeouts during a failure.
  • AP: always responsive, but you must design for stale reads and conflicts (for example "last write wins", or merging).

The best interview habit: do not say "I pick CP". Say "for this feature, wrong data is worse than no data, so during a partition I would refuse writes (CP). For that other feature, I would stay up and fix it later (AP)."

Quick quiz

What do C, A and P stand for?

Consistency (reads see the latest write), Availability (every working node answers), Partition tolerance (system keeps working when the network between nodes breaks).

Why is partition tolerance not really optional?

Networks between machines do fail. A distributed system that cannot survive that is not usable, so the real choice is between C and A during a partition.

What does PACELC add to CAP?

It covers the normal case: if there is no partition, you still trade latency against consistency, because waiting for replicas to agree takes time.

Is "AP" the same as "data gets lost"?

No. Replicas may disagree for a short time, then converge after the partition heals. You must design for stale reads and conflicts.

Your app has a wallet balance and a "trending now" list. What would you choose for each, and why?

Wallet: CP, a wrong balance can lose money, so I would reject or delay during a partition. Trending: AP, a slightly old list is harmless and staying responsive matters more.

Say it in an interview

"Partitions will happen, so the real choice is consistency or availability while one is happening. For payments I choose consistency; for likes I choose availability."
"Even without a partition there is a latency versus consistency trade-off, which is the PACELC extension."

Common follow-up question: Is your whole system CP or AP?

Neither as a whole. I decide per feature. The ledger and inventory checkout are CP, because a wrong answer costs money. The feed, counters and last-seen are AP, because being up and fast matters more than being exact.
